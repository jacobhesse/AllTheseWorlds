NIST SP 800 — What It Is and How to Use It

The NIST 800 series is the U.S. government’s most widely adopted body of cybersecurity guidance. Below you’ll find crisp summaries of the publications that matter most, who they’re for, what artifacts to produce, and blunt guidance on when each actually moves the needle.

At a glance

Start here if you just need the right document for the job. Click any tile to expand the plain‑English summary, deliverables to produce, and our take on when it’s worth the effort.

SP 800‑53 Rev. 5
Security & Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations (2020, upd. 1)
What it coversControls Catalog

800‑53 is the authoritative U.S. catalog of security and privacy controls. It’s the menu you pick from to manage risk, typically alongside SP 800‑53B for baselines and the Risk Management Framework (RMF).

Produce: System Security Plan (SSP), control tailoring decisions, control implementation statements, POA&Ms, and continuous monitoring plan.

Our take: For non‑federal orgs, treat 800‑53 as a reference library and use CSF 2.0 profiles or ISO 27001 controls as your organizing spine. Bring 53 in when auditors or contracts point to it.

Reference: NIST SP 800‑53r5 and SP 800‑53B.

SP 800‑171 Rev. 3
Protecting CUI in Nonfederal Systems (Final: May 2024)
What it coversCUI Safeguards

Makes the security requirements you must meet when your organization handles Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) under federal contracts. Assessed using the companion SP 800‑171A Rev. 3.

Produce: CUI boundary, current score, SSP, POA&M, and evidence mapped to 110 requirements; plan for enhanced requirements if SP 800‑172 applies.

Our take: If you sell to DoD or other agencies, 171 is non‑negotiable. Start with gap analysis → build your SSP from real configs, not policy fiction → track close‑out dates like a program, not a doc set.

Reference: NIST SP 800‑171r3 and SP 800‑171A r3.

SP 800‑172
Enhanced Security Requirements for CUI (2021)
What it coversAdvanced Protections

Extra safeguards for CUI tied to high value assets or critical programs—think adversaries with time and budget. Not everyone needs this, but when you do, it’s decisive.

Produce: Overlay of enhanced requirements on top of 171 controls, with supporting design docs (segmentation, cryptographic protections, anomaly detection, provenance).

Reference: NIST SP 800‑172.

SP 800‑61 Rev. 3
Computer Security Incident Handling Guide (2025)
What it coversIR Playbooks

The fresh 2025 revision updates incident response concepts, roles, and lifecycle activities and aligns explicitly with CSF 2.0 outcomes and 800‑53 controls.

Produce: IR policy, role charters, severity schema, comms & legal runbooks, playbooks for top threats (ransomware, business email compromise, supply chain), and an exercise schedule with after‑action learning.

Our take: Treat IR as an always‑on capability, not a binder. Integrate detection tooling, ticketing, and post‑incident changes into one loop.

Reference: NIST SP 800‑61r3 (r2 withdrawn April 2025).

SP 800‑207 & 207A
Zero Trust Architecture (2020) + Access Policy Model (2023)
What it coversArchitecture

Defines Zero Trust principles and components, then adds a model for enforcing granular, application‑level access policies. Pairs well with identity hardening (SP 800‑63‑4).

Produce: Current‑state ZT map, target policy decision points, identity & device trust signals, micro‑segmentation plan, and migration backlog.

Our take: Don’t “boil the ocean.” Start with one high‑value app and mandate policy decisions based on identity, device, and context.

Reference: NIST SP 800‑207 and SP 800‑207A.

SP 800‑63 Rev. 4
Digital Identity Guidelines (Final: July 2025)
What it coversIdentity

Sets assurance levels and requirements for identity proofing, authentication, and federation. Rev. 4 modernizes guidance for phishing‑resistant MFA, provenance, and risk‑based proofing.

Produce: Identity assurance profile, authenticator inventory, MFA rollout plan, federation trust framework, and relying‑party policy.

Reference: NIST SP 800‑63‑4 suite.

SP 800‑30 & 800‑37 Rev. 2
Risk Assessment + Risk Management Framework (2012/2018)
What it coversRisk & RMF

How to conduct risk assessments (800‑30) and operationalize risk management throughout the system lifecycle (800‑37). The backbone behind control selection and authorization.

Produce: Risk assessment report with likelihood/impact, RMF plan, authorization package, and continuous monitoring strategy.

Reference: NIST SP 800‑30r1, SP 800‑37r2.

SP 800‑137
Information Security Continuous Monitoring (2011)
What it coversISCM

Strategy and program design for continuous security monitoring. Still the canonical reference for metrics, data sources, and decisioning.

Produce: ISCM strategy, metric catalog, data pipelines, dashboards, response thresholds, and governance cadence.

Reference: NIST SP 800‑137.

SP 800‑34 Rev. 1
Contingency Planning for Federal Information Systems (2010)
What it coversBC/DR

Classic guidance for contingency planning and system recovery. Old but useful—especially to shape tabletop scenarios and recovery priorities.

Produce: Business impact analysis, recovery objectives (RTO/RPO), system contingency plans, exercise schedule, and lessons‑learned loop.

Reference: NIST SP 800‑34r1.

SP 800‑122
Guide to Protecting the Confidentiality of PII (2010)
What it coversPII

Plain‑language privacy guardrails for identifying and protecting PII, including impact levels and breach response considerations. Pairs well with ISO 27701 and your privacy program.

Produce: PII inventory & data maps, confidentiality impact levels, minimization policy, privacy notices, and incident response playbook for PII.

Reference: NIST SP 800‑122.

SP 800‑82 Rev. 3
Guide to Operational Technology (OT) Security (Final: 2023)
What it coversOT/ICS

End‑to‑end security guidance for industrial and building control systems, with an OT overlay to 800‑53r5. Critical for manufacturing, utilities, and facilities.

Produce: OT asset inventory, zone & conduit diagram, safety‑aware segmentation policy, and compensating controls where patching is constrained.

Reference: NIST SP 800‑82r3.

SP 800‑218 (± 218A)
Secure Software Development Framework v1.1 (2022) + AI Profile (2025)
What it coversSecure SDLC

Outcome‑based practices for building and buying software that’s secure by default. Community profile 218A adds guidance for generative‑AI and dual‑use models.

Produce: SSDF policy, practice‑to‑task matrix, secure build pipelines, SBOMs, vuln disclosure program, and supplier requirements based on SSDF.

Reference: NIST SP 800‑218 and the SSDF project page.

SP 800‑160 Vol. 1 (Rev. 1)
Systems Security Engineering (2018, updated 2022)
What it coversEngineering

How to bake security into engineered systems from concept through disposal—bridging risk, architecture, and assurance cases. Ideal for complex, safety‑critical, or regulated systems.

Produce: Security requirements derived from mission threads, architectural decisions with risk rationale, and verification evidence tied to assurance claims.

Reference: NIST SP 800‑160 Vol. 1 r1.

SP 800‑115
Technical Guide to Security Testing & Assessment (2008)
What it coversTesting

Foundational guidance for planning and running technical tests—from vuln scans to pen tests—and turning findings into remediation plans.

Produce: Assessment plan, rules of engagement, data‑handling plan, test logs/evidence, and consolidated findings with risk treatment.

Reference: NIST SP 800‑115.

How this maps to your program

Use 800‑53/53B as the control catalog, 800‑30/37 to manage risk and authorizations, and 800‑137 to keep the loop running. If you handle CUI, layer 800‑171 (and possibly 800‑172) on top. For identities and access, apply 800‑63‑4 and 800‑207. For operational resilience, round it out with 800‑61r3 and 800‑34r1. Building software? Anchor on 800‑218 and 800‑160.

Stargazer POV: Don’t implement documents—implement behaviors. Start with a risk‑based profile (CSF 2.0), pull only the controls that mitigate your top risks, and let evidence drive your SSPs and audits.
