How it works

The work is organized around outcomes, not meetings. You get a predictable operating cadence, written artifacts, and a clear decision pipeline so security doesn’t devolve into “best effort.”

1) Diagnose what matters

Establish a clear baseline: current controls, real operational behaviors, customer expectations, compliance targets, and where the business is taking risk without realizing it.

2) Build the operating model

Define decision rights, risk acceptance, exception handling, control ownership, and the cadence for ongoing posture management.

3) Execute with governance

Drive a prioritized roadmap with evidence and accountability. Avoid “checkbox security” and focus on controls you can sustain.

FAQ

If you are evaluating vCISO support, these are the questions that usually determine fit.

Do you replace a full-time CISO?

For many SMB and mid-market teams, yes—especially when the immediate need is executive risk governance, audit readiness, incident preparedness, and a working security program. If you have a complex environment requiring daily operational security management, we can structure this engagement to build the program while you recruit.

Will you “run tools” for us (SIEM, EDR, IAM platforms)?

Typically no. The role is fractional security leadership: posture, governance, priorities, and accountability. I will coordinate with your IT/engineering teams and vendors, define requirements, and validate outcomes. If you need hands-on engineering support, I can help you scope and manage it.

What compliance standards do you support?

Common scopes include SOC 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA-adjacent programs, and customer due diligence requirements. The emphasis is on “controls you can sustain,” with evidence and ownership that does not degrade after the audit.

How quickly can we be ready for enterprise customer security reviews?

Often within 30–60 days you can materially improve readiness by clarifying posture, hardening the narrative, establishing an evidence model, and fixing high-impact gaps. Timing depends on your current state and the review’s rigor.

How do we measure success?

Reduced unmanaged risk, increased audit readiness, improved decision clarity, shorter customer security review cycles, and a security program that continues to function month-over-month. You should be able to explain your posture in one page, defend your risk acceptances, and show continuous improvement without fire drills.
